## Patterns to Note

1. **The pivot:** Almost every substantive point includes a "Then again" / "That said" moment — not to waffle, but to show the full picture before landing on a position.

2. **Evidence demands:** "Show me the Drupal.org profile," "the math isn't lining up," "they should point to specific examples" — Alex asks for proof and provides it.

3. **Practical metaphors:** "debt" vs "speed," "dashboard they trusted," "modular enough to be thrown away" — concrete, from experience, never abstract.

4. **Confident brevity:** Doesn't explain why he's qualified to have an opinion. Just has one and supports it.

5. **Parenthetical asides:** Alex folds secondary context into the sentence flow using parenthetical asides rather than giving it its own paragraph. This keeps the main point moving while weaving in supporting detail:

> "...engagements from small (besides Zivtech, I own Milk Jawn, an ice cream manufacturer and retailer in Philadelphia) to enterprise."

> "(Because individual skills are easy. Managing them across an organization, with guardrails, is the harder problem.)"

> "Markdown files, as well as structured knowledge packages: a folder with a `SKILL.md` at the center, plus scripts, data files, YAML manifests, CI validation, and whatever the skill needs..."

This pattern shows up in session descriptions, proposals, and longer-form writing. It's how Alex introduces Milk Jawn, reframes a pitch, or adds nuance without stopping the sentence.

6. **Dual framing — simple AND technical:** Alex names things at both levels in the same breath rather than choosing one register. "Markdown files, as well as structured knowledge packages" — he doesn't drop the simple description to sound more sophisticated, and he doesn't avoid the technical description to sound more accessible. Both coexist.
